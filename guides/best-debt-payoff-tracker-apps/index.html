Best debt payoff tracker apps (2026)

Apps that put your loans, EMIs, credit cards and IOUs in one place and tell you when you'll be debt-free — compared on price, ads, payoff methods, currencies, and whether they need your bank login or your SMS.

Updated 16 October 2026 · By Technook

Short answer. Want loans, EMIs, credit cards and money you've lent in one free app with no ads and no SMS access? Dawnpath. Want the most payoff methods and a long track record? Debt Payoff Planner. Prefer a website to an app? Undebt.it. Want a full budget, not just a debt plan? YNAB — or Monarch Money if you're in the US or Canada.

A disclosure first: we make Dawnpath, one of the apps below. We've tried to make this genuinely useful. Every fact about the other apps was checked against its Google Play or App Store listing, official website or help centre on 16 October 2026, and we say plainly where Dawnpath falls short.

Dawnpath — best free all-in-one, built for EMIs and IOUs

Good for: anyone juggling several loans, a card or two and some informal borrowing, especially in India, who doesn't want an app reading their messages.

Dawnpath keeps every kind of debt in one place: home, car, personal, education and gold loans, pay-later plans and informal IOUs, plus money you've lent to family and friends, tracked beside what you owe. Its Journey screen shows a projected debt-free date, how much you've paid and how far is left, and the date moves when a payment changes. Scenarios shows how much sooner you'd finish, and how much interest you'd save, by paying extra each month with the avalanche or snowball method.

Credit cards, app credit lines, overdrafts and pay-later limits get their own tab with two charts — what you borrowed and what it cost in interest — and a gentle flag if you've been borrowing to repay. Regular payments are assumed paid so you don't log each one, and the app periodically asks you to confirm. Debts can be in any of 150+ currencies, with totals in your home currency at exchange rates you set.

  • Free, no ads, no in-app purchases
  • Requests no SMS permission and never connects to your bank
  • Money you've lent, tracked beside what you owe
  • Payment reminders and home-screen widgets with amounts you can hide

Watch out for: it's brand new (launched October 2026) with very few downloads and no ratings yet, and it's Android only — no iPhone or web app. You need an account, because your data is stored under it so it follows you to a new phone. Because nothing is imported, you have to keep balances up to date yourself.

Debt Payoff Planner — the established choice

Good for: people who want a proven, single-purpose payoff planner and lots of strategy options.

Made by OxbowSoft, Debt Payoff Planner & Tracker has 500K+ downloads and a 4.5 rating on Google Play. You enter each debt's balance, interest rate and minimum payment, then choose snowball, avalanche, "snowflake" one-off extra payments or your own custom order, and it shows your debt-free date. Credit cards are one of eight debt categories.

Watch out for: the Play listing says it contains ads and in-app purchases. Pro starts at $2 a month on its website; its help page confirms multi-device access, biweekly payments and other minimum-payment methods are Pro features. Multi-currency support and tracking money owed to you aren't mentioned.

Undebt.it — best if you'd rather use a website

Good for: planners who like a big screen and want to try many payoff methods.

Undebt.it is a website, not a Play Store app — its FAQ suggests adding it to your home screen instead. The free calculator works with manual entry and no account. Undebt.it+ costs $10 a year as a one-off annual fee, with a 30-day trial and no card needed, and unlocks all eight payoff methods, what-if scenarios, a target date, bill tracking, and bank sync through SimpleFIN or YNAB.

Watch out for: hiding ads is listed as a Plus feature, which suggests the free tier shows them. Currency support is display formatting by country, not conversion between currencies.

YNAB — when you want a whole budget

Good for: people who want to change how they budget every rupee or dollar, with debt payoff as one part of it.

YNAB (You Need A Budget) has no ads, a 34-day free trial, and 1M+ downloads with a 4.6 rating on Google Play. Its Loan Planner shows how extra payments shorten a loan.

Watch out for: it costs $14.99 a month or $109 a year. Bank import covers only select US, Canadian, UK and EU banks; elsewhere you use manual entry or file import. Each plan uses one currency. The Loan Planner works on one loan at a time, its simulator is web only, and YNAB itself says to keep credit cards off the Loan Planner. No snowball or avalanche planning is mentioned.

Monarch Money — strong, but not for India

Good for: US and Canadian households who want everything synced automatically from their banks.

Monarch (4.8 rating, 500K+ downloads) syncs credit cards and loans, including minimum payments due, through third-party bank data providers, and promises no ads. Plans are $14.99 a month or $99.99 a year after a 7-day trial.

Watch out for: its help centre says it is only available in the US and Canada, shows everything in dollars and does no currency conversion. It isn't an option if your loans are in India.

IOU trackers and EMI reminders

If you mainly need to remember who owes whom, a few smaller apps focus on that:

  • Debt Tracker: IOU & Money Owed — 100K+ downloads, 4.5 rating. Tracks money lent and owed in multiple currencies, offline, with no account. Contains ads; no payoff plan or debt-free date.
  • Debt Manager and Tracker (Century Egg) — 500K+ downloads, 4.1 rating, multi-currency, money owed to and by you. Contains ads, Pro is $1.49. Last updated in August 2022.
  • EMI Reminder & Loan Tracker — small (5K+ downloads), no ads, covers gold loans and credit card EMIs and estimates a debt-free date.

Khatabook and OkCredit come up in searches too, but they are ledgers for businesses tracking customer credit, not personal debt planners.

Looking for a Tally alternative? Tally, the credit card payoff app, shut down in 2024 after ending its consumer app that April. Any of the planners above can replace its payoff tracking; none of them lends you money the way Tally did.

Snowball vs avalanche, briefly

Both methods pay the minimum on every debt and put any extra money towards one of them.

  • Avalanche targets the highest interest rate first. It costs the least in total interest.
  • Snowball targets the smallest balance first. It clears whole debts sooner, which keeps many people motivated.

The best app is one that shows you both with your own numbers, so you can see what the difference really is before you choose.

Frequently asked questions

Which app can track all my EMIs and loans in one place?

Dawnpath tracks home, car, personal, education and gold loans, credit cards, pay-later plans and informal IOUs together, and shows a projected debt-free date. It is free, has no ads and does not need SMS access. Debt Payoff Planner is a well-established alternative with more payoff methods, but it shows ads and some features need Pro.

Is there a debt payoff app that doesn't link to my bank or read my SMS?

Yes. Dawnpath, Debt Payoff Planner and Undebt.it's free calculator all work from numbers you type in. Dawnpath requests no SMS permission at all and never connects to a bank.

Snowball or avalanche: which is better?

Avalanche (highest interest rate first) saves the most money. Snowball (smallest balance first) clears whole debts sooner, which many people find easier to stick with. Dawnpath, Debt Payoff Planner and Undebt.it can all show both.

Is there an app to track money I lent to friends?

Dawnpath tracks money you've lent beside what you owe. Debt Tracker: IOU & Money Owed and Debt Manager and Tracker focus on IOUs only, with ads, and neither calculates a payoff plan.

Does Monarch Money or YNAB work in India?

Monarch Money's help centre says it is only available in the US and Canada. YNAB can be used anywhere with manual entry or file import, but bank import covers only select US, Canadian, UK and EU banks, and it costs $14.99 a month or $109 a year.
