Best shared shopping list apps for Android (2026)

Seven apps compared for one job: a grocery list that you and your partner, flatmates or family can all edit, live. What's actually free, which ones show ads, what premium costs, and who each one suits.

Updated 16 October 2026 · By Technook

Short answer. Both on Android and want it free with no ads? OneList. One of you on an iPhone and you're happy to pay a little? AnyList. Want the biggest free feature list and don't mind ads? Listonic or Bring!. Already live in Google's apps and only need checkboxes? Google Keep.

A disclosure before anything else: we make OneList, one of the apps below. We've tried to be useful rather than flattering. Every fact about the other six apps was checked against its Google Play listing, App Store listing or official website on 16 October 2026, and we say plainly where OneList falls short.

OneList — best free option with no ads

Good for: couples and families who are all on Android and want shared lists without ads, subscriptions or a sign-up wall.

OneList is built around one idea: a single list that lives on everyone's phone. Changes appear on the other phones as you make them, every item shows who added it, and the app warns you when something is already on the list, so nobody buys milk twice. You invite someone with a 6-letter code, not a phone number or email, and you can use the app without an account until you want to share.

In the shop, items sort themselves into aisles, Buy again keeps your regulars one tap away, and store mode gives you big rows with the screen kept on. You can see when your partner is shopping so you don't both go. Beyond groceries there are wishlists with price-drop alerts and gift lists where family can quietly claim presents. It's in English, Hindi and six other languages.

  • Free, no ads, no subscription, no in-app purchases
  • Live sync, who-added-what, duplicate warnings, "Sam is shopping now"
  • Gift lists with claiming, wishlists with price-drop alerts
  • Works offline and syncs when you're back

Watch out for: it's Android only, so it won't work if your partner has an iPhone. It's also new, from a small studio, with few downloads and no ratings yet. There are no recipes, meal planning or store loyalty cards.

Listonic — the most features for free

Good for: people who want a lot of features without paying and don't mind ads.

Listonic has been around for years and it shows: 10M+ downloads and a 4.6 rating from over 300,000 reviews on Google Play. Lists share in real time, and its website says you don't need an account to share — a free account is only for syncing across your own devices and for backup. It sorts items by supermarket category and has voice input, prices with a running total, a recipe keeper, a pantry check, a widget and an AI assistant that takes text, voice or a photo. It is available in more than 40 languages, including Hindi, and on almost every platform.

Watch out for: the Play listing says it contains ads, and the in-app purchase range is wide (₹25–₹4,750 in India), with several premium tiers. Premium removes ads and adds themes.

Bring! — best for recipes and aisle order

Good for: households who cook from recipes and want the list in the same order as their store.

Bring! (10M+ downloads, 4.1 rating) syncs in real time and notifies you when someone changes the list. You can rearrange its categories to match your store's aisles, import recipes from websites, see offers from nearby stores and keep loyalty cards in the app. It also runs on Wear OS and Apple Watch.

Watch out for: the free version contains ads. Sharing requires an account, and you invite people by their account email. On the US App Store, Premium is $1.99 a month or $8.99 a year.

AnyList — best if one of you has an iPhone

Good for: mixed Android-and-iPhone households, and anyone who plans meals.

AnyList has the highest rating here (4.8 on Google Play from about 31,000 reviews) and no "Contains ads" label. The free plan covers lists and real-time sharing. Items sort into aisles you can reorder, and it has widgets, Alexa and Siri support and recipes.

Watch out for: a lot sits behind AnyList Complete, which costs $9.99 a year for one person or $14.99 a year for a household on its official pricing page: prices with a running total, the meal-planning calendar, photos, and the web, desktop and watch apps. The free plan allows only five recipe imports from the web. The person you invite needs to create an AnyList account with the email you invited.

OurGroceries — simplest for a whole family

Good for: families who want everyone on exactly the same lists with the least setup, on any device.

OurGroceries (1M+ downloads, 4.7 rating) gives you every feature in its free, ad-supported version. Changes show up on other phones within seconds. It sorts by category or aisle and has barcode scanning, photo-to-item, recipes, a pantry list, and support for Alexa, Siri and Google Assistant. It runs on Android, iPhone, the web, Wear OS, Apple Watch and Echo.

Watch out for: sharing works by everyone signing in to the same OurGroceries account, rather than each person having their own. Ads appear at the bottom of the screen unless you pay a one-off fee to remove them (₹85–₹990 on Play in India). Its regional category suggestions don't include India.

Google Keep — good enough if you only need checkboxes

Good for: people who already use Google's apps and want a shared checklist, nothing more.

Keep is free with no ads, has more than a billion downloads, and syncs reliably. Make a checklist, then add collaborators by email or share it with your Google family group. It has reminders, widgets and voice notes.

Watch out for: Keep is a notes app, not a shopping app. It won't sort items into aisles, total up prices, remember what you buy every week, or tell you that someone is already at the shop. Long lists get messy quickly.

Out of Milk — a solid veteran

Good for: people who want a pantry list and running totals along with the shopping list.

Out of Milk (5M+ downloads, 4.5 rating) says that creating lists, syncing and sharing are completely free. It groups items by category, scans barcodes, keeps pantry and to-do lists, and shows a grand total and running total as you shop.

Watch out for: the free version contains ads, and sharing needs an account, with invites sent by email. There is no Android in-app purchase listed to remove ads.

How to choose

  1. Check everyone's phone first. If anyone is on an iPhone, rule out Android-only apps such as OneList. AnyList, OurGroceries, Listonic, Bring!, Keep and Out of Milk all work on both.
  2. Decide how you feel about ads. OneList, Google Keep and AnyList's free plan show no ads. Listonic, Bring!, OurGroceries and Out of Milk do unless you pay.
  3. Count the sign-ups. Bring!, AnyList and Out of Milk need an account for each person you share with. OneList uses an invite code; OurGroceries uses one shared login.
  4. Pick the extras you'll really use. Recipes and meal plans point to AnyList or Bring!. Gift lists and wishlists point to OneList. Barcode scanning points to OurGroceries or Out of Milk.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best app to share a grocery list with my partner?

If you both use Android and want it free with no ads, OneList is built for exactly this: live sync, who-added-what on every item, and a 6-letter invite code. If one of you has an iPhone, AnyList and OurGroceries are strong cross-platform choices; Listonic and Bring! are free with ads and work everywhere.

Is there a free shopping list app with no ads that syncs with family?

Yes. OneList is free with no ads and no in-app purchases. Google Keep is free with no ads but has no grocery features. AnyList's free plan carries no ads label on Google Play, though prices, meal planning and desktop apps need its paid Complete plan.

Can I use Google Keep as a shared grocery list?

Yes. Make a checklist note and add collaborators by email or share it with your Google family group. It syncs reliably, but it won't sort items into aisles, track prices or remember what you buy regularly.

Is Listonic really free?

Listonic's core features are free, but its Google Play listing says it contains ads and offers in-app purchases. Premium removes ads and adds themes.

Which shopping list app sorts items by store aisle?

OneList, Listonic, Bring!, AnyList, OurGroceries and Out of Milk all group items by category or aisle. AnyList and Bring! let you reorder categories to match your own store. Google Keep does not sort items.
