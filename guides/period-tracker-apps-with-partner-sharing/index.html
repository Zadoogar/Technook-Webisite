Period tracker apps that share with your partner (2026)

Some trackers let a partner, your mother or a friend see your cycle. They differ a lot in what that person sees, whether you get to choose, how many people you can share with, and what happens to your data along the way.

Updated 16 October 2026 · By Technook

Short answer. Want to choose exactly what each person sees — everything for your partner, dates only for your mother? Ova. Want the biggest, most polished tracker with a simple partner view? Flo or Clue, which share a fixed set of cycle days. Want nothing to ever leave your phone? Euki or drip, which have no sharing at all.

A disclosure first: we make Ova, one of the apps below. Because this is health data, we've linked the official help pages and independent privacy reviews behind each claim so you can check them yourself. Everything was checked on 16 October 2026.

Ova — best if you want to choose, person by person

Good for: anyone who wants different people to see different things, and wants to see who looked.

Ova was built around one question: who else gets to see this? Each share is separate, and for each person you turn on cycle dates, symptoms, intimacy and mood as four separate switches, with a plain-words line explaining what each one reveals. Give your partner everything, your mother the dates only, and a friend nothing but a heads-up that your period started. Any share can be paused or ended instantly.

Two details stand out. Predictions are never shared: people see the days you logged, never Ova's estimate of your fertile window, and that's enforced on the server rather than just hidden in the app. And looking is visible both ways: you can see when each person last opened your data, and they can see when you opened theirs.

  • No ads, no analytics, no third-party trackers; Play's data safety section says no data collected or shared
  • Four modes: trying to conceive, cycle awareness, pregnancy, postpartum and baby
  • Basal temperature from Health Connect, with the post-ovulation shift marked
  • A one-page PDF summary for a doctor's appointment

Watch out for: Ova is new, with few downloads and no ratings yet, Android only and in English only. Because sharing needs a server, your logs are stored online under your account (you can delete everything, including the server copy, at any time); if you want data that never leaves your phone, choose Euki or drip. Like every app here, it is not contraception.

Flo — the biggest, with a simple partner view

Good for: people who want the most popular, feature-rich tracker and a straightforward partner calendar.

Flo has 100M+ downloads and a 4.7 rating on Google Play, plus Health Connect, basal temperature and a pregnancy mode. Flo for Partners links a partner (18+) with a pairing code. They get a view-only cycle calendar and insights; they can't see your logged symptoms or personal notes and can't edit anything. You can stop sharing with one tap. Flo's help centre says basic partner access is free and Premium adds content.

Watch out for: the shared set is chosen by Flo, not you, and it's one partner. Flo's own help centre says Anonymous Mode can't be used with Flo for Partners. On privacy history, the US Federal Trade Commission finalised an order in 2021 over Flo sharing health data with Facebook and Google; Flo now requires consent before such sharing. Mozilla's 2026 privacy review scored it 7 out of 10.

Clue — science-led, sharing with one person

Good for: people who want a well-regarded tracker with careful consent settings and only need to share with one person.

Clue (50M+ downloads, 4.5 rating) has basal temperature, wearable sync, and pregnancy and perimenopause modes. Clue Connect shares your period, fertile, ovulation and PMS days in a calendar; mood, energy and pain are never shared. You can remove the connection at any time. Mozilla scored Clue 8 out of 10, noting that consent is split by purpose and can be changed later.

Watch out for: sharing your cycle needs Clue Plus (viewing someone else's is free). It's one person at a time, one direction, and not available in Pregnancy mode, and you can't choose what's in the shared set.

Stardust — social, with privacy questions

Good for: people who like a playful, astrology-flavoured tracker and want to sync with friends.

Stardust (1M+ downloads, 4.5 rating) has Partner Mode and Friend Sync using a cycle code, and you can remove or block a partner at any time. Its paid tier, Stardust Super, adds horoscopes, tarot and a symptom library. We couldn't find documentation of how granular its sharing is.

Watch out for: Mozilla's 2026 review scored Stardust 2 out of 10, reporting symptom details sent to an analytics service with a persistent ID, and device and advertising-ID data sent to AppsFlyer and Facebook. That sits awkwardly with its Play listing's "No data shared with third parties".

Maya — popular in India, partner sync just added

Good for: Indian users who want a familiar, full-featured tracker with backup across devices.

Maya, by Plackal Tech, has 10M+ downloads and a 4.5 rating. It has backup and multi-device sync, temperature logging, a pregnancy mode and a biometric lock. Its latest release notes mention "seamless partner sync", but we couldn't find what that shares.

Watch out for: it contains ads, and its Play data safety section declares sharing device IDs for advertising. In 2019, Privacy International found it sending contraception, mood and diary data to Facebook; Plackal said it removed Facebook's analytics SDKs (MediaNama).

Period Calendar — huge, simple, ad-funded, no sharing

Period Calendar by Simple Design (100M+ downloads, 4.9 rating) needs no account, supports multiple profiles on one phone, backs up to Google and charts basal temperature. It has no partner sharing. It is ad-funded; Mozilla scored it 6 out of 10, finding device data sent to ad networks but no health data shared.

Euki and drip — most private, nothing shared

If privacy matters more than sharing, these two are the strongest choices. Euki is a nonprofit, open-source app that keeps data on the device only, with no account, a PIN lock and scheduled data wipes; Mozilla gave it 10 out of 10. drip is also open source and on-device, supports temperature-based fertility awareness, and its Play listing shows no data collected or shared. Neither has any sharing feature or cloud backup.

Is there anything else that lets you choose?

PairCare says it shares with a "partner, family member, or someone you trust" and that you choose what to share, with "flexible sharing controls" in Premium. It contains ads. We couldn't confirm how granular those controls are, so check before relying on it.

Questions to ask before you share

  1. Exactly what will they see? Cycle days only, or symptoms, mood and intimacy too? Can you change it later?
  2. Will they see predictions? A predicted fertile window shared as fact can mislead. Ova never shares predictions; check what other apps do.
  3. Can you share differently with different people? Most apps share the same set with one person.
  4. How do you stop? Every app here lets you end sharing; make sure it takes effect immediately.
  5. What happens to your data? Look for ads, analytics and third-party trackers, not just "we don't sell your data".

Frequently asked questions

Can my boyfriend or partner see my period tracker?

Only if you share it. Flo, Clue, Stardust and Ova all have a sharing feature you have to switch on, and all let you stop sharing. What the other person sees differs: Flo and Clue share a fixed set of cycle days chosen by the app, while Ova lets you choose cycle dates, symptoms, intimacy and mood separately for each person.

Which period tracker lets me choose what my partner sees?

Ova is built around this: every share is separate, and cycle dates, symptoms, intimacy and mood are four separate switches per person. Flo for Partners and Clue Connect share a fixed set chosen by the app. PairCare says you can choose what to share, but we couldn't confirm how granular that is.

Is Clue Connect free?

Viewing someone else's cycle through Clue Connect is free, but sharing your own cycle needs Clue Plus. You can connect with one person at a time, and it isn't available in Pregnancy mode.

Can I share my period dates with my mother but not my symptoms?

In Ova, yes: create a separate share for her with only cycle dates switched on. Most other trackers share the same fixed set with everyone, or allow only one connection.

What is the most private period tracker app?

If you want data that never leaves your phone, Euki and drip are open source and store everything on the device; Mozilla rated Euki 10 out of 10. They have no sharing. If you want sharing, Ova has no ads, no analytics and no third-party trackers, but your data is stored on a server so it can be shared.
